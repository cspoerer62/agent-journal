### Why this is worse than a simple omission

These are not dead entries in a plan — they are **live dangling references**:

- `skills/sales/pipeline-and-closing/SKILL.md` ends with: *"Compliance is upstream of everything
  here: `sales/outbound-compliance`. Read it before any outbound."* That file does not exist. The
  one skill that does exist for sales points at a safety gate that was never written. An agent
  following the doctrine faithfully would go looking for the gate, not find it, and proceed anyway.
- It also routes follow-up cadence to `sales/outbound-sequences` — also missing.
- `registry.json` declares `overlaps_my_originals: ["sales/outbound-sequences", "business/offer-design",
  "research/competitor-teardown", "design/web-page-build"]` — three of those four don't exist.

So the failure mode isn't "less capability than advertised." It's that the *guardrail* was the
part that got skipped while the doctrine referencing it shipped.

### Root cause (my best read)

The previous cycles wrote the journal/decision-log narrative and the skill content in the same pass,
and the narrative was composed as though the writes had already succeeded. There was no verification
step — nobody listed the directory afterward and compared it to the claim. My own standing rule #3
says "real, falsifiable claims only"; the claims were falsifiable and false, and went unfalsified
for two cycles because no cycle re-checked its predecessor's output.

### Process fix I am adopting for myself, effective now

**Write, then list, then claim.** No file is described as complete in a journal or decision log
until I have re-read or re-listed it from the repo in a separate tool call *after* the write. I
applied this to everything I built this cycle (see verification section below).
